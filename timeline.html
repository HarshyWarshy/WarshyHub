Timeline Mastery

Detailed AP World Timeline + Essay-Ready Evidence

Use this page as a high-detail review pass: each period includes key developments, named evidence, and suggested analytical angles for SAQ/DBQ/LEQ writing.

1200–1450 1450–1750 1750–1900 1900–Present

1200–1450: Global Tapestry + Networks of Exchange

Key Developments

  • Song China, Dar al-Islam states, and regional empires consolidated governance.
  • Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, and trans-Saharan routes intensified Afro-Eurasian exchange.
  • Commercial practices (diaspora merchants, caravanserai, credit systems) expanded trade reliability.
  • Cultural and religious diffusion accelerated through merchants and travelers.

Evidence Bank

  • Mansa Musa’s hajj and Mali’s role in trans-Saharan gold trade.
  • Zheng He’s voyages and state-sponsored maritime diplomacy.
  • Spread of Islam in East Africa and South/Southeast Asia via commerce.
  • Black Death transmission through interconnected Eurasian trade routes.

Useful LEQ/DBQ Angle

Argue continuity and change by noting exchange grew in volume and distance while still depending on established regional intermediaries and local trade hubs.

1450–1750: Land-Based Empires + Transoceanic Interconnections

Key Developments

  • Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal empires expanded through gunpowder military capacity.
  • European maritime empires reshaped global commerce and created Atlantic systems.
  • Columbian Exchange transformed demography, environment, and food regimes.
  • Chattel slavery and plantation economies expanded under transatlantic demand.

Evidence Bank

  • Janissary system and Ottoman administrative centralization.
  • Potosí silver and integration of American extraction into global trade.
  • Triangular trade routes linking Europe, Africa, and the Americas.
  • Akbar’s state-building and selective religious accommodation in Mughal India.

Useful LEQ/DBQ Angle

For comparison prompts, show how empires shared goals (taxation, legitimacy, expansion) but differed in religious policy, military recruitment, and regional integration.

1750–1900: Revolutions + Industrialization + Imperialism

Key Developments

  • Atlantic revolutions challenged old hierarchies and spread new political ideals.
  • Industrialization shifted labor, production, urbanization, and energy systems.
  • Imperial states intensified extraction and political control in Africa/Asia.
  • Mass migration and contract labor systems linked colonies to global markets.

Evidence Bank

  • Haitian Revolution and anticolonial implications.
  • Meiji reforms as selective modernization under external pressure.
  • Berlin Conference and formalized partition of African territories.
  • Railroads/telegraphs as tools for imperial logistics and governance.

Useful LEQ/DBQ Angle

For causation, connect industrial demand to imperial expansion while noting ideology (social Darwinism, nationalism) as a force multiplier rather than sole cause.

1900–Present: Global Conflict, Decolonization, and Globalization

Key Developments

  • World wars altered borders, political legitimacy, and state capacity.
  • Cold War competition shaped alliances, development models, and proxy wars.
  • Decolonization produced formal sovereignty alongside new structural constraints.
  • Globalization accelerated capital, media, migration, and supply-chain integration.

Evidence Bank

  • Non-Aligned Movement and postcolonial diplomatic balancing.
  • IMF/World Bank development frameworks and dependency critiques.
  • Green Revolution and regional food production shifts.
  • EU and other regional blocs as experiments in supranational governance.

Useful LEQ/DBQ Angle

For evaluation prompts, balance gains in political sovereignty against continued economic and geopolitical dependence in postcolonial contexts.
