1200–1450: Networks of Exchange

Big Developments

  • Long-distance trade intensified across Afro-Eurasia.
  • Mongol rule increased exchange, movement, and transmission of ideas and disease.
  • Commercial cities and diasporic merchant communities expanded.
  • Belief systems spread through trade and missionary contact.

Evidence Bank

  • Mansa Musa and trans-Saharan gold trade.
  • Caravanserai and improved trade safety on Silk Roads.
  • Zheng He voyages and Ming maritime projection.
  • Swahili Coast city-states linking Africa and Indian Ocean trade.

Exam Angle

For CCOT prompts, argue that trade scale changed dramatically while reliance on intermediaries and regional specializations continued.

1450–1750: Gunpowder States + Oceanic Expansion

Big Developments

  • Land empires expanded bureaucracies and military systems.
  • European maritime empires established Atlantic-centered trade routes.
  • Columbian Exchange shifted demography and agriculture globally.
  • Coerced labor systems expanded in the Americas.

Evidence Bank

  • Ottoman devshirme and Janissary corps.
  • Potosí silver feeding global trade circuits.
  • Sugar plantation economies and transatlantic slavery.
  • Mughal administration and religious policy under Akbar.

Exam Angle

For comparison LEQs, compare empire goals (taxation, stability, expansion) and then contrast policy tools and social effects.

1750–1900: Revolutions, Industry, Imperial Power

Big Developments

  • Industrialization transformed production and transportation.
  • Political revolutions redefined citizenship and sovereignty.
  • New imperialism intensified extraction and racial hierarchy.
  • Mass migration and labor restructuring reshaped societies.

Evidence Bank

  • Haitian Revolution and anti-colonial implications.
  • Meiji Restoration industrial modernization.
  • Berlin Conference and partition of Africa.
  • Railroads and telegraph as imperial control tools.

Exam Angle

For causation prompts, connect industrial demand to imperial conquest while discussing political ideology and nationalism as accelerants.

1900–Present: Conflict, Independence, and Interdependence

Big Developments

  • World wars produced new political orders and institutions.
  • Cold War rivalries shaped alliances and development policy.
  • Decolonization created sovereign states amid structural constraints.
  • Globalization integrated finance, trade, and communication.

Evidence Bank

  • Non-Aligned Movement strategies.
  • Postcolonial economic dependency debates.
  • Green Revolution agricultural transformation.
  • EU integration and regional governance experiments.

Exam Angle

For evaluation prompts, weigh political independence against continued economic and geopolitical dependency.
