AP World Timeline: Key Developments + Exam Cues

Use this page for fast unit refresh and thesis prep. Each era includes major events, useful context, and writing angles you can reuse in SAQs, DBQs, and LEQs.

1200–1450 1450–1750 1750–1900 1900–Present

1200–1450: Networks of Exchange

Core Developments

  • Silk Roads expanded with caravanserai and new technologies.
  • Indian Ocean trade intensified via monsoon knowledge and port cities.
  • Trans-Saharan trade linked West Africa with Mediterranean markets.
  • Mongol Empire increased overland connectivity and disease transmission.

Useful Evidence

  • Mansa Musa’s hajj and Mali gold wealth.
  • Zheng He voyages as a state-sponsored maritime projection.
  • Spread of Islam and Buddhism through trade routes.

Essay Angle

Explain continuity/change in trade by arguing that exchange grew in scale while still depending on older regional merchant diasporas.

1450–1750: Land-Based Empires + Maritime Expansion

Core Developments

  • Gunpowder empires (Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal) consolidated power.
  • European maritime empires expanded in the Atlantic and Indian Oceans.
  • Columbian Exchange transformed diets, population, and ecology.
  • Atlantic slave trade restructured labor systems across continents.

Useful Evidence

  • Devshirme and janissaries in Ottoman governance.
  • Silver flow from Potosí to Ming/Qing China via global trade.
  • Plantation systems in the Americas and coerced labor systems.

Essay Angle

Compare empires by showing similar centralization goals but different methods of religious policy, military recruitment, and local governance.

1750–1900: Industrialization, Revolutions, and Empire

Core Developments

  • Industrial Revolution changed production and energy systems.
  • Political revolutions spread ideas of rights and nationalism.
  • Imperial powers intensified colonial extraction and control.
  • Migration surged through indentured labor and urbanization.

Useful Evidence

  • Haiti’s revolution as anticolonial and anti-slavery turning point.
  • Meiji reforms as state-led modernization.
  • Berlin Conference and scramble for Africa.

Essay Angle

Build a causation thesis linking industrialization to both stronger states and deeper social inequality in colonies and industrial cities.

1900–Present: Global Conflict, Decolonization, Globalization

Core Developments

  • World Wars transformed borders, governance, and technology.
  • Cold War created ideological and proxy conflicts.
  • Decolonization produced new states and postcolonial challenges.
  • Globalization accelerated through trade blocs and digital networks.

Useful Evidence

  • Non-Aligned Movement and sovereignty debates.
  • Green Revolution, development policy, and demographic change.
  • European Union as supranational integration model.

Essay Angle

Evaluate whether decolonization produced true autonomy by balancing formal independence against economic dependency and Cold War pressures.
